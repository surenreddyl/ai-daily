## The idea

Most AI use is about producing things: drafts, summaries, tables. An equally valuable use is **thinking**. AI can be a tireless sparring partner that argues the other side, plays your toughest stakeholder or imagines how your plan could fail.

There's one catch: AI assistants tend to be **agreeable**. Ask "Is this a good plan?" and you'll often hear yes. You have to invite the challenge.

## Why it matters

- **Honest challenge is rare.** Teams often hesitate to criticise a leader's plan. AI has no such hesitation if you ask.
- **It's fast and private.** You can stress-test a half-formed idea at 10pm before showing anyone.
- **Better decisions, fewer surprises.** Objections you hear in rehearsal are objections you can answer in the meeting.

## How it works

Four techniques, each a single prompt:

1. **Devil's advocate.** "Argue against this plan as strongly as a smart sceptic would."
2. **Premortem.** "It's a year from now and this failed. Why?" This technique comes from psychologist Gary Klein: imagining failure as a fact makes risks easier to see and say.
3. **Stakeholder role-play.** "You are the client's CFO. Here's my proposal. What worries you?" Then reply and keep the conversation going.
4. **Questions, not answers.** "Before you give any advice, ask me the five questions a great consultant would ask about this decision."

Always finish with: **"What would change your view?"** and **"What am I missing?"**

## Real-world example

*An illustrative scenario.* A sales director is preparing a large renewal where the client is pushing on price. She pastes an anonymised summary into her company's approved assistant and asks it to play the client's procurement lead.

In ten minutes of role-play, the "client" raises an objection she hadn't considered: switching costs are lower than she assumed, because a competitor offers free migration. She prepares a response and a fallback offer, and goes into the real meeting ready for it.

**The lesson for leaders:** treat AI as the colleague who always tells you the uncomfortable truth, but only when asked. Then apply your own judgement: AI challenges your thinking; it doesn't replace it.
