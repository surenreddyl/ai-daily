## The idea

Four general AI assistants dominate work use: **Claude** (Anthropic), **ChatGPT** (OpenAI), **Gemini** (Google) and **Microsoft Copilot**. All four can draft, summarise, analyse and brainstorm well. Each has free and paid tiers, and each improves every few months.

For everyday business tasks the gap between them is smaller than the headlines suggest. **How well you use one matters more than which one you pick.**

## Why it matters

- **Your people are already choosing.** If you don't set a default, everyone picks their own, often on personal accounts with consumer terms.
- **Fit beats features.** An assistant that sits inside your email and documents saves the copy-and-paste step dozens of times a day.
- **Skill compounds.** A team that learns one tool well shares prompts and tips; a team on five tools shares little.

## How it works

Choose in three steps:

1. **Start with what's approved.** Ask IT or legal which tools may handle company data. Business and enterprise plans from the major vendors generally commit not to train on your company's data by default. Confirm what your agreement says.
2. **Match where your work lives.** Microsoft 365 shops get the most from Copilot; Google Workspace shops from Gemini. Claude and ChatGPT work well as stand-alone assistants and also connect to many tools.
3. **Test on your own tasks.** Run two or three real tasks through your shortlist with identical prompts. Score accuracy, usefulness and editing time. Pick a default; keep a second for when the first struggles.

## Real-world example

Microsoft and LinkedIn's 2024 **Work Trend Index** surveyed 31,000 people in 31 countries. It found that 75% of knowledge workers were already using generative AI at work, and that 78% of those users were bringing their own AI tools rather than using company-provided ones.

**The lesson for leaders:** adoption is happening with or without you. Choosing an approved default, and teaching people to use it well, turns scattered experiments into a shared capability, and keeps company data on terms you control.
