## The idea

Long documents (contracts, RFPs, board papers, proposals, research reports) arrive faster than anyone can read them properly. AI can act as a **fast first reader**: summarising, comparing and flagging what deserves your attention.

It builds directly on Day 6, **working from your material**. The difference here is scale: dozens or hundreds of pages at once.

## Why it matters

- **Hours become minutes.** A 60-page RFP can be summarised into requirements and deadlines before your coffee cools.
- **Comparisons get fair.** Side-by-side tables stop the best-written proposal from winning by default.
- **Risks surface early.** Asking AI to flag unusual clauses points you to the pages that matter.

## How it works

1. **Upload, following your rules.** Use an approved assistant for confidential documents (Day 5). Most assistants accept PDFs and Word files. Claude Projects and Google's notebook tool let you work across several documents at once.
2. **Ask for a structured summary.** "One page: purpose, key points, numbers, risks, decisions needed. Give a page or section reference for each point."
3. **Compare side by side.** "Compare these three proposals in a table: scope, team, timeline, cost, gaps."
4. **Flag, then read.** "List anything unusual or high-risk I should read in full." Then read those sections yourself. For contracts, involve legal for anything material.

## Real-world example

*An illustrative scenario.* A B2B bid team receives a 90-page tender with a five-day deadline. They upload it to their approved assistant and ask for: mandatory requirements, evaluation criteria and weights, deadlines, and anything they can't meet, each with page references.

Within ten minutes they have a requirements checklist. AI flags an unusual clause requiring a local data centre, which the team confirms on page 47 and escalates immediately. The team spends its five days writing a strong response instead of reading. A retail buyer uses the same approach to compare supplier contracts before a negotiation.

**The lesson for leaders:** let AI read first and point; let people read what matters and decide.
