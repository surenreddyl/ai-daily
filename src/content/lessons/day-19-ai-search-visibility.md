## The idea

Buyers used to type a few words into Google and click through links. Increasingly they ask an AI assistant a full question, such as "What's the best CRM for a 50-person services firm?", and get a written answer that **names a few brands**.

If AI systems can't find, understand or trust your information, you're not in that answer. Marketers are starting to call the work of fixing this **generative engine optimisation (GEO)**.

## Why it matters

- **The shortlist forms earlier.** If an AI answer names three competitors and not you, many buyers never visit your site.
- **AI repeats what it reads.** Outdated pages, unclear descriptions or old reviews can shape what AI says about you.
- **Fundamentals still matter.** Google says the same SEO foundations apply to its AI features. The good news: clarity helps everywhere.

## How it works

1. **The buyer asks.** A natural question with context: size, budget, location, need.
2. **AI reads public sources.** Your website, reviews, news, comparison articles, forums.
3. **It answers and names brands,** often linking to the sources it relied on.
4. **Be clear, cited and trusted.** Say plainly what you sell, who it's for and what it costs (or a range). Back claims with specific facts and sources. Earn mentions on trusted third-party sites.

Google's guidance is reassuring: there are **no special requirements** to appear in its AI Overviews or AI Mode. Pages must be indexed and eligible for a snippet, and the usual advice on helpful, people-first content applies.

## Real-world example

In 2023, a team of academic researchers published **"GEO: Generative Engine Optimization"**, later accepted at the KDD 2024 conference. They tested ways of rewriting content to see what made AI-generated answers more likely to feature it. Methods like **adding statistics, quotations and citations** improved visibility, by **up to 40%** in their experiments.

The lesson isn't to stuff pages with numbers. It's that AI systems, like busy humans, favour content that is specific, credible and easy to quote.

**The lesson for leaders:** run a simple AI-visibility check every quarter, using your customers' real questions, B2B and consumer alike. Fix what's unclear before producing more content.
