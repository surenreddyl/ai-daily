## The idea

Many teams have no clear rules on AI. The result is confusion: some people avoid AI for fear of breaking rules; others use it in risky ways without realising. A **one-page team AI policy**, aligned with your company's wider policies, solves both problems by giving people **permission and boundaries**.

You now have everything you need to write one. This lesson pulls Weeks 1–8 together.

*This lesson is general information, not legal advice. Check your draft with your legal or compliance team.*

## Why it matters

- **Confidence.** People use AI more, and better, when they know what's allowed.
- **Protection.** Clear data, checking and high-risk rules prevent the mistakes you've seen in this course.
- **Alignment.** Governments are setting direction. India's **AI Governance Guidelines** (November 2025, under the IndiaAI Mission) are built on the principle "Do No Harm". The EU AI Act and frameworks like the US **NIST AI Risk Management Framework** offer global reference points.

## How it works

Use this one-page template. Keep each section to a few lines.

> **1. Purpose.** We use AI to work faster and better, safely and responsibly.
>
> **2. Approved tools.** Which assistants we use (e.g. our company Copilot or Gemini, approved ChatGPT/Claude business accounts), and for which data.
>
> **3. Data rules.** Green: fine to use. Amber: anonymise first. Red: never enter (customer personal data in unapproved tools, passwords, unreleased financials).
>
> **4. Check and disclose.** We verify numbers, names, quotes and claims before anything leaves the team. A named reviewer signs off on client and regulator work. We disclose AI use when clients or rules require it.
>
> **5. High-risk uses.** People make final decisions on hiring, pay, credit, legal and safety matters. Customer-facing AI needs approval from [owner].
>
> **6. Owner and review.** [Name] answers questions. We review this policy every six months.

**Align, don't duplicate.** Check it against your company's IT, legal and data policies, and link to them.

## Real-world example

*An illustrative scenario.* A national sales director in Delhi notices her 40-person team using five different AI tools, some on personal accounts, with no shared rules. She drafts a one-page policy using this template in an afternoon, checks it with IT and legal, and walks the team through it in a 30-minute call.

Within a month, everyone uses the approved assistant, client data stays in company tools, and proposals get a fact check before sending. Unexpectedly, AI use goes *up*: people who'd held back now know what's allowed.

**The lesson for leaders:** a short, clear policy is one of the highest-impact AI actions you can take this month. Write it, share it, review it.
