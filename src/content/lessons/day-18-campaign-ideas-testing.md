## The idea

Great campaigns usually come from **many ideas** and **honest testing**. Both used to be expensive: brainstorms took days, and producing several creative versions took budget.

AI makes ideas and variants almost free. It can generate dozens of angles, headlines and concepts in minutes. That shifts the valuable skills to **choosing well** and **testing properly**.

## Why it matters

- **More options, better odds.** The best idea is rarely the first one. AI widens the funnel.
- **Testing gets affordable.** More variants mean you can let customers, not opinions, pick winners.
- **Faster learning.** Small, quick tests teach you what your audience responds to, campaign after campaign.

## How it works

1. **Go wide.** Give AI the audience, goal and key benefit. Ask for 15–20 very different angles: rational, emotional, surprising, humorous, fear-of-missing-out.
2. **Set criteria first.** Before reading the ideas, decide how you'll judge them: on-brand, clear benefit, different from competitors, feasible.
3. **Shortlist and develop.** Pick three, then ask AI for headline, image and copy variants of each.
4. **Test with real customers.** Run a simple **A/B test**: two versions to comparable audiences, one clear measure (clicks, sign-ups, sales). Scale the winner.

**Tip:** ask AI to run a premortem on your favourite idea (Day 9): "Why might this campaign flop with our audience?"

## Real-world example

In 2023 **Coca-Cola** launched "Create Real Magic", a platform built with OpenAI and Bain & Company that combined GPT-4 and the image generator DALL-E. It invited digital artists to create artwork using iconic Coca-Cola brand assets, with the chance to have their work featured on Coke's digital billboards in New York's Times Square and London's Piccadilly Circus.

Coca-Cola didn't just use AI to make ads faster. It used AI to generate a huge volume of creative ideas from outside the company, then selected the best for its highest-profile placements.

**The lesson for leaders:** use AI to widen the range of ideas, keep human judgement for selection, and let real audiences decide what scales.
