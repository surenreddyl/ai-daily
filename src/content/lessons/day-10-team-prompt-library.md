## The idea

Over the past two weeks you've written prompts that work: a call prep prompt, a one-page brief, a voice card. Right now they probably live in your chat history, where nobody else can find them, including you next month.

A **prompt library** is a shared, organised home for your team's best prompts. It's the simplest way to turn individual AI skill into team capability.

## Why it matters

- **Skill spreads.** The best prompt in the team becomes everyone's prompt.
- **Quality becomes consistent.** Everyone's updates, briefs and drafts follow the same proven shape.
- **New joiners ramp faster.** They start with what works instead of learning from scratch.

## How it works

1. **Write it well.** Use what you've learned: context, task, format, example (Day 3), your material (Day 6), voice (Day 7) and structure (Day 8).
2. **Test it.** Run it on two or three real cases. If you have to fix the output every time, fix the prompt instead.
3. **Save it in one shared place.** A shared document works fine. Many assistants also let you save instructions and files for reuse: **Projects** in Claude, **custom GPTs** in ChatGPT and **Gems** in Gemini.
4. **Share it, then improve it.** Tell the team when to use it. Ask for feedback. Review the library monthly and retire what nobody uses.

Each entry needs five parts: **name**, **when to use it**, **the prompt with [BLANKS]**, **an example output**, and **an owner**.

## Real-world example

*An illustrative scenario.* A regional sales team of twelve noticed that two reps consistently walked into meetings better prepared. Both had built their own call prep prompts.

The manager asked them to merge the best of both into one library prompt. It now sits in the team's shared AI workspace with blanks for the account notes and the meeting goal. Every rep uses it before key calls, and the weekly pipeline review starts with what the prompt surfaced.

**The lesson for leaders:** you don't need an AI programme to start. Ask your team for their best prompt this week, put the winners in one place, and name an owner. That's a library, and it's the start of an AI-capable team.
