## The idea

AI makes it easy to send more messages. That's the trap. Buyers are flooded with AI-written outreach that inserts their name into a generic template, and they ignore it.

The opportunity is the opposite: use AI to find a **genuine reason** to contact each person, and to draft a **short message in your voice**. Then a human checks every one.

## Why it matters

- **Relevance wins replies.** A message about something real in the buyer's world stands out from the flood.
- **Volume can backfire.** More generic emails mean more unsubscribes, spam complaints and damage to your domain's reputation.
- **Trust is fragile.** One invented "fact" about a prospect ends the conversation before it starts.

## How it works

1. **Pick a tight segment.** People with the same role, the same problem and the same buying moment. Smaller and sharper beats bigger.
2. **Find one real hook each.** A post they wrote, a product launch, a new office, a hiring push, a public review. AI can help you scan sources quickly, but the hook must be real.
3. **Let AI draft, within your rules.** Paste your voice card (Day 7), the hook, and one problem you solve. Ask for three sentences, under 80–90 words, ending in a low-pressure question.
4. **Edit before sending.** Read every message. Remove anything you can't stand behind.

**Follow the rules.** Consent, honesty and opt-out rules (such as CAN-SPAM in the US and the UK's electronic marketing rules) apply however the email was written. Check your company's policy and local law.

## Real-world example

*An illustrative scenario.* A B2B software team sends 400 templated emails a week with a 1% reply rate. They switch to 60 emails a week: AI finds a recent hook for each account and drafts a short note, and each rep spends a minute editing.

Replies rise and unsubscribes fall. Reps say conversations start warmer because the first line was about the buyer, not the product. A consumer brand applies the same idea to win-back emails, personalising by what each customer actually bought, never by invented details.

**The lesson for leaders:** measure replies and meetings, not emails sent. Use AI to raise relevance, not just volume.
