## The idea

AI can draft copy, create images and write code in seconds. That raises questions businesses didn't face before: **Who owns AI-generated content? Can it be protected? Could it infringe someone else's work?**

The honest answer: the law is **still settling**, in India and worldwide. Leaders need sensible habits now, and a good relationship with their legal team.

*This lesson is general information, not legal advice.*

## Why it matters

- **Ownership.** If your logo or campaign is mostly AI-generated, you may not be able to stop others copying it.
- **Infringement risk.** AI outputs can sometimes resemble existing works closely.
- **Cases are live.** Courts are deciding whether training AI on copyrighted content is lawful, including in India.

## How it works

1. **Copyright needs human creativity.** In its January 2025 report, the **US Copyright Office** concluded that AI-generated outputs can be protected only where a human author has determined sufficient expressive elements. Creative arrangement or modification can qualify; **prompts alone don't**. Other countries, including India, are still working through these questions, so check local advice.
2. **Training data is contested.** In India, news agency **ANI** sued OpenAI in the **Delhi High Court** in November 2024, alleging its content was used to train AI without permission. The case was still pending as of mid-2026.
3. **Transparency rules are growing.** The EU AI Act includes transparency and copyright-related obligations for providers of general-purpose AI models.
4. **Practical habits:** add real human creative input to key brand assets; never ask AI to copy a specific creator's or competitor's work; check important outputs for close resemblance; keep simple records of how key work was made; and ask legal to review vendor terms on ownership and indemnity.

## Real-world example

*An illustrative scenario.* A Bengaluru-based consumer brand uses AI to generate dozens of logo concepts for a new product line. The design lead picks one AI concept as a starting point, then substantially redraws it: new lettering, custom shapes, the brand's own colour system.

Legal advises keeping the design files showing the human work, and running a quick check that the final logo doesn't resemble existing marks before filing for trademark protection. The team gets AI's speed in exploring ideas, and a final asset with clear human authorship.

**The lesson for leaders:** use AI to explore, then let people create what you need to own. When in doubt, ask legal early, not after launch.
