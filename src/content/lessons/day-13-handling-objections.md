## The idea

Most deals don't die on surprises. They stall on the same few objections: price, timing, "we're happy with our current supplier", "I need to check with my boss". The difference between average and great sellers is often **preparation**: having a clear, confident answer ready.

AI makes rehearsal easy. It can predict the objections for a specific deal, **play the buyer**, and **score your answers**, as many times as you like, with no judgement.

## Why it matters

- **Confidence shows.** Buyers notice hesitation. A rehearsed answer sounds calm and credible.
- **Fewer stalled deals.** Objections handled well in the meeting don't turn into weeks of silence.
- **Coaching at scale.** Managers can't role-play with every rep before every call. AI can.

## How it works

1. **Map the objections.** Describe the deal in a few anonymised sentences and ask: "List the five most likely objections, ranked."
2. **Role-play the buyer.** "Play their procurement lead. Raise these one at a time and push back on my answers."
3. **Score and improve.** "After each answer, score it 1–5 and show me a stronger version." Ask for honesty: AI tends to be generous (Day 9).
4. **Know your walk-away point.** Negotiation experts call it your **BATNA**: your best alternative to a negotiated agreement. Ask AI to help you define it before you go in.

**Tip:** rehearse out loud. Many assistants have a voice mode, so you can practise the real conversation, not just typing.

## Real-world example

*An illustrative scenario.* A key-account manager at a packaging supplier faces a renewal where the customer wants a 12% price cut. She asks AI to play the customer's procurement director.

In three rounds, the "buyer" pushes on a competitor's lower quote, the cost of switching and payment terms. Her first answers score 2 out of 5; by the third round she has a clear value story, a smaller concession on payment terms instead of price, and a walk-away point. In the real meeting the price objection comes up exactly as rehearsed, and she holds her ground.

**The lesson for leaders:** make "rehearse with AI" a normal part of preparing for big deals and hard conversations, for consumer complaints as well as B2B negotiations.
