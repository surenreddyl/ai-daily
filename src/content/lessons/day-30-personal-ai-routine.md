## The idea

You're halfway through AI Daily. You've learned what AI is, how to prompt it, and how to use it in sales, marketing, analysis and everyday work. The difference between knowing and benefiting is **habit**.

Today is about building a **personal AI routine**: a simple daily rhythm, **saved instructions** so your assistant always knows your context, a **weekly review**, and clear rules for **when not to use AI**.

## Why it matters

- **Habits beat bursts.** Using AI a little every day builds skill faster than occasional big experiments.
- **Context saves time.** Saved instructions mean you stop re-explaining your role and style in every chat.
- **Judgement protects you.** Knowing when not to use AI is part of using it well.

## How it works

1. **Plan (morning).** Ask your assistant to summarise what needs you today (Day 26) and help pick your top three priorities.
2. **Draft (during the day).** Use your saved prompts (Day 10) for your three most common tasks: emails, briefs, analysis, meeting prep.
3. **Capture (end of day).** Save any prompt that worked well into your library.
4. **Review (weekly).** Fifteen minutes on Friday: what saved time, what didn't, what to try next week.

**Save your context.** Set up **custom instructions** in ChatGPT, a **Project** in Claude, or a **Gem** in Gemini with your role, priorities and preferred style. Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace users can do similar things in Copilot and Gemini.

**Know when not to use AI.** Wharton professor Ethan Mollick lists situations where AI can be counterproductive: when you need to **learn** something new yourself, when **very high accuracy** is required, when you don't understand how AI might fail, and when **the effort is the point**, such as a heartfelt message or real feedback.

## Real-world example

*An illustrative scenario.* A general manager notices her AI use is sporadic: brilliant one day, forgotten the next. She writes a one-page routine: a morning inbox summary, saved prompts for board updates and supplier emails, and a Friday review.

She sets up custom instructions describing her business, her role and her writing style, and lists three things she won't use AI for: performance feedback, condolence notes and final contract wording. After a month, her Friday reviews show she's saving about five hours a week, and she has a short list of prompts her team now uses too.

**The lesson for leaders:** don't just use AI. Build a routine, review it weekly, and share what works with your team.
